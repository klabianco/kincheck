KinCheck

A paper card that proves who's on the phone. A cloned voice can sound exactly like your grandson, but it can't read his card. No app, no account, no internet, and no phone needed by the person who's most likely to be targeted. Read the protocol.

  1. Print a card for every pair of people. Grandma gets a card for calls with Alex and another for calls with Sam. Each pair has its own 30 lines, and they exist only on paper.
  2. Asked for money in a hurry? Ask for a check. Take out the card with the caller's name: "Card check: line 1, maple." They have to read back that line's two words.
  3. Cross the line off. Both of you do. A scammer who overheard it gets nothing, because that line never works again.

Make your family's cards

One name per line, up to 8. Each pair of people gets its own card, one page per person: 3 people need 6 pages, 4 people need 12.

Printed at the top so cards are easy to recognise.

Nothing leaves this page. It makes no network requests (the browser blocks them), and it saves nothing, not even on this computer. Once you close the tab, the printed cards are the only copies.

What it stops, and what it doesn't

A cloned voice calls Grandma asking for bail moneyStops it They can't read back line 1. The chance of guessing two words from 256 is about 1 in 65,000.
A scammer overheard a past checkStops it Every line is used once and then crossed off. An overheard line is already dead.
A scammer calls Alex pretending to be Grandma: "read me line 4 so I know it's you"Stops it The person asking always says the ask word first. Without it, you don't answer.
A scammer picks up an ask word in a failed check, then uses it on another relativeStops it Every pair of people has its own card. An ask word Grandma said to a fake "Alex" works only on the Grandma ↔ Alex card, on a line she has already crossed off. It opens nothing on Sam's cards. This is also why a correct ask word alone doesn't prove who's calling: if they then ask you for something, run your own check.
Someone guesses which line you'll pickStops it There is nothing to guess. You always use your top line that isn't crossed off, and each line's words are random.
A scammer relays a check live between two real family members, at the same momentCan't stop it This is rare and hard to pull off. For anything big, hang up and call back on the number you already have saved.
The card is lost, stolen or photographedCan't stop it Make new cards for every pair that card covered, and shred the old ones. Keep the card in a drawer, not on display. A photo on a cloud-backed phone is a copy too.

How it works underneath: the same idea as the paper authentication tables militaries have used on voice radio for decades, and the one-time code lists banks used to post to customers, turned around for families. Words come from your browser's cryptographic random generator (crypto.getRandomValues). A 256-word list means each word is one random byte, and the words were picked to be hard to mishear on a bad line. Rhymes like rocket / pocket were removed, and a script checks shared word endings every time the page is built.

Before you rely on it: rehearse once

Hand out the cards, then do a five-minute pretend call with whoever is most likely to be targeted. One person plays the "grandkid in trouble" and is in a hurry. The other practises saying "Card check: line 1, …" and hanging up when the answer is wrong. People under stress fall back on habits they've practised, not on paper. Cross off line 1 afterward, as you would on a real call.
